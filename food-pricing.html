Arundel Cellars & Brewing Co. · Board meeting 11/17/26

Raise food prices about 15%

Move the $15 items to $17 and raise the rest by a similar amount. This adds about $28,000–$35,000 profit for every 10 months of sales.

Why we need to do this

60.7%Food cost today (% of food sales)
23.4%Kitchen labor (% of food sales)
$36.6KLeft from $230.7K food sales, Jan–Oct
52.7%Food cost after the raise

Food plus kitchen labor uses about 84 cents of every food dollar. A normal target for food cost alone is 30–35%. Our prices have not kept up with our costs.

Because each plate costs so much to make, we could lose about 30% of food orders and still make the same profit as today. Most guests come for the wine; food is an add-on.

Lunch specials: raise to $13 or stop

Each $15 sandwich costs about $9.10 in food and $3.50 in kitchen labor, about $12.60 total. At $10, every special loses about $2.60. The specials gave away about $4,900 in discounts from January to October.

Toast does not show the specials bringing in extra drink sales. On special days and on weekends (no specials), guests buy about $2 of drinks for every $1 of food. Monday and Thursday have the same sandwich special, but Monday lunch sold $27.2K and Thursday only $22.0K.

Next steps

  1. Board approves the new prices.
  2. Update Toast, the printed menus and the website menu together. The website is missing some items, like Chicken Nachos and Dip Duo.
  3. Start in the slow season so prices are set before spring.
  4. Ask what is booked to the QuickBooks "Food" cost account. Paper goods, cleaning supplies, staff meals or event food may be in there. September was 44%, so some months may have extra costs.
  5. Enter item costs in Toast so we can see profit for each menu item.
